Korea Replaced Its Toxic Substance List With Three Hazard Categories. How 2,451 Substances Sort Out

October 21, 2026 · K-REACH: Regulations, K-REACH: Chemical Safety

Until August 2025, Korean chemical law kept a single list of toxic substances. A mixture became a toxic substance once a listed ingredient passed a set concentration, and that concentration was low enough to catch everyday products. Under the old rules, a mixture containing 0.1 percent benzene counted as toxic, which put gasoline, at roughly 0.7 percent benzene, under the same controls as far more dangerous materials.

On August 7, 2025, the revised Act on the Registration and Evaluation of Chemicals (K-REACH) replaced that single category with three hazard categories, each with its own concentration threshold for mixtures. We counted every designation in the current public data to see where the substances ended up.

What the three categories mean

Human acute hazard covers substances that can harm health after a single or short exposure. Human chronic hazard covers harm from repeated exposure, or harm that shows up after a latency period. Ecological hazard covers harm to aquatic organisms and the wider environment from short or long exposure. English summaries of the law translate the collective term in several ways; this post calls the group hazardous substances.

Each designation lists a threshold for every category that applies to the substance. A mixture containing the substance above that concentration falls into that category. Because one substance can carry a different threshold for each category, a mixture can fall into one category and stay out of another.

Substances that were on the old toxic list kept their identification numbers and were carried over automatically. Where a new threshold was lower than the old one, the lower threshold applied from January 1, 2026.

How the substances sort

Our database holds 2,451 substance records with at least one of these designations. That is more than the number of designation entries, because a group entry such as selenium and its compounds is followed in the source data by a separate record for each member substance it names.

For most substances the strictest acute threshold is 1 percent and the strictest chronic threshold is 0.1 percent, but two go lower. Bis(2-chloroethyl)ether (CAS 111-44-4) has an acute threshold of 0.1 percent, and PCBs (CAS 1336-36-3) have a chronic threshold of 0.005 percent.

Counting at those levels or below, 827 substances have at least one threshold of 1 percent or less for acute hazard, 0.1 percent or less for chronic hazard, or 0.1 percent for ecological hazard. For these substances, a minor component of a formulation can be enough to bring the whole mixture under the rules.

Group entries and their members

Most group entries that list member substances have no CAS number of their own, so a search by CAS number usually lands on a member record. The member record does not always repeat the group's thresholds.

The entry for inorganic silver salts lists an acute threshold of 10 percent and an ecological threshold of 25 percent. Three of its named members, silver nitrate (7761-88-8), disilver(1+) sulfate (10294-26-5) and sulfuric acid silver salt (19287-89-9), keep the 10 percent acute threshold but carry an ecological threshold of 1 percent, so a check against the group line alone would put the ecological limit for silver nitrate 25 times too high.

Antimony trifluoride (7783-56-4) has an acute threshold of 25 percent, while the group entry for inorganic antimony compounds, which excludes a few named antimony oxides, lists 10 percent. Calcium nitrite (13780-06-8) has no ecological threshold, although the group for nitrous acid salts lists one at 25 percent.

The 2026 additions

Notice 2026-5 of the National Institute of Chemical Safety, issued on March 27, 2026, added 74 entries numbered 2026-1-1281 to 2026-1-1354. It took effect on July 1, 2026, except for sodium hypochlorite, whose designation takes effect on January 1, 2028.

When the draft was published in December 2025, the institute explained that the revised law had widened the skin corrosion criterion from Category 1A to all of Category 1 (1A, 1B and 1C), and that substances already reviewed were being checked again against it. A skin corrosion finding is an acute effect, and the new entries line up with that.

Sixty-nine of the 74 entries were in our database when we ran these counts on October 21, 2026. Of those, 68 are designated for acute human hazard only, and none for chronic hazard. Across all 69, the acute threshold is 10 percent for 67 entries and 5 percent for two. The one entry with a second category is sodium hypochlorite (7681-52-9), the active ingredient in household bleach, which also carries an ecological threshold of 2.5 percent. Four of the 69 are listed without a CAS number, so a screen that relies on CAS numbers alone will not find them.

The notice gives businesses that were already handling the newly designated substances more time under the Chemicals Control Act. Hazard labeling and import declarations are due by January 1, 2027, and business licensing and reporting by July 1, 2028.

Checking a substance

Search by CAS number and read the record that comes back, not the group above it. Note each category with its threshold and compare them with the concentration in your mixture. Then check the effective date, since a substance can appear on the list before its designation applies, as sodium hypochlorite does until 2028. If you handled any of the 2026 additions before July 2026, the labeling date to plan for is January 1, 2027.
